San Jose to Kings Canyon & Sequoia National Park

An end-to-end 3-day exploration of the world's largest living trees, dizzying granite canyon rims, and sub-alpine fall colors. Compare 3 distinct lodging strategies to match your pace and budget.

🚗 Distance: ~480-540 mi round-trip
🌡️ Temp: Day 55°F–65°F | Night 30°F–40°F
🏔️ Elevation: 1,300 ft to 7,200+ ft

Trip Quick Health Check

  • ✓ Route: Counter-clockwise loop (North Entry CA-180 → South Exit CA-198) prevents repetitive back-tracking.
  • ⚠️ Cedar Grove Status: Kings Canyon Scenic Byway closes by late Oct; mid-October is often the last open weekend.
  • ⚠️ Tire Chains: California highway law requires carrying chains in mountain passes beginning mid-October.
  • ✓ Entrance Pass: 1 pass ($35/vehicle or America the Beautiful Pass) covers both parks for 7 days.

Curated 3-Day Route

Hour-by-Hour Travel Itinerary

ROUTE FOCUS: San Jose → Big Stump Entrance → Grant Grove → Kings Canyon Scenic Byway (Cedar Grove)
⏱️ 5.5 hrs total driving 📍 Distance: ~235 mi ⛰️ Peak Alt: 6,600 ft
06:30 AM – 10:30 AM Driving: ~3.5 – 4.0 hrs (200 mi)

Depart San Jose → Fresno Fuel Stop → Big Stump Entrance

Take US-101 S to CA-152 E over Pacheco Pass, then CA-99 S to Fresno. Top off fuel and pick up deli sandwiches/trail water in East Fresno or Sanger before ascending CA-180 E into the mountains.

10:30 AM – 12:30 PM Walking: 1.0 mi (Easy loop)

Grant Grove: General Grant Tree & Fallen Monarch

Explore the General Grant Tree (the nation's Christmas Tree and 2nd largest tree by volume on Earth). Walk right through the hollowed interior of the historic Fallen Monarch tree trunk used as a stable in the 1800s.

12:30 PM – 04:30 PM Driving + Scenic Stops: ~45 mi

Kings Canyon Scenic Byway (CA-180) to Cedar Grove & Zumwalt Meadow

Carve deep into North America’s deepest river gorge (deeper than Grand Canyon at points). Stop at Junction View for sheer rock vistas, view the gushing Roaring River Falls, and stroll the serene boardwalk trail at Zumwalt Meadow framed by massive granite walls.

05:00 PM – 07:00 PM Check-in & Dinner

Evening Arrival at Lodging

Option A: Stay at Grant Grove’s John Muir Lodge or Montecito-Sequoia Lodge. Option B: Drive south on Generals Highway to Three Rivers town for dinner at Gateway Restaurant. Option C: Descend back to Fresno/Clovis.

ROUTE FOCUS: Giant Forest → General Sherman Tree → Congress Trail → Moro Rock → Tunnel Log
⏱️ 2.5 hrs scenic driving 📍 Elevation: 6,800 – 7,100 ft 🥾 ~4.5 mi walking
08:30 AM – 11:30 AM Walking: 2.5 mi loop

General Sherman Tree & Congress Trail Loop

Descend the paved trail to stand before the General Sherman Tree, the world's largest living organism by volume (~52,500 cu ft). Continue onto the tranquil Congress Trail to walk beneath clusters known as "The Senate" and "The House".

11:45 AM – 01:15 PM Visitor Center & Picnic

Giant Forest Museum & Sentinel Tree

Learn why these monarchs only grow on the western Sierra slope at 5,000–7,500 ft elevation. Pick up souvenirs, inspect the massive Sentinel Tree directly out front, and enjoy a packed picnic lunch.

01:30 PM – 03:30 PM Drive-through & Meadow walk

Tunnel Log & Crescent Meadow

Drive your vehicle through the famous Tunnel Log (a giant fallen sequoia carved out in 1937). Continue to Crescent Meadow, dubbed the "Gem of the Sierra" by John Muir, where deer and black bears frequently forage.

03:45 PM – 05:30 PM Stairs: 350 steps climb

Moro Rock Granite Monolith (Golden Hour Panorama)

Climb the exhilarating 350-step stone stairway carved into the spine of Moro Rock. Revel in a 360-degree vista of the Great Western Divide and the steep canyon below before sunset.

06:00 PM – Night Descent to South Entrance

Lodging in Three Rivers

Descend the Generals Highway switchbacks into Three Rivers. Enjoy hot food and craft cider/beer (Sierra Smokehouse BBQ or River View Restaurant).

ROUTE FOCUS: Three Rivers → Foothills / Hospital Rock → Visalia Lunch → Return to San Jose
⏱️ 4.0 hrs return drive 📍 Distance: ~235 mi 🛣️ Route: CA-198 W → CA-99 N
09:00 AM – 10:45 AM Low Altitude: 2,700 ft

Hospital Rock & Foothills Historical Exploration

Stop at Hospital Rock along the Kaweah River to view Native American pictographs and bedrock mortar holes used by the Monache people. Enjoy pleasant low-elevation warmth among California live oaks and buckeyes.

11:00 AM – 12:30 PM Scenic exit & Farm country

Exit via Ash Mountain Entrance → Downtown Visalia Lunch

Pass through the historic wooden Indian Head entrance arch. Drive 35 minutes west to charming downtown Main Street in Visalia for lunch (e.g., Pita Kabob or Brewbakers).

01:00 PM – 05:00 PM Highway Driving: ~200 mi

Highway 99 N to Pacheco Pass (CA-152) → San Jose

Cruising north on CA-99 and west over CA-152 Pacheco Pass. By departing around 1 PM, you comfortably reach San Jose before heavy Sunday evening Bay Area traffic jams.

Visual Inspiration

Iconic Sights Along Your Route

Sequoia National Park 7,000 ft

General Sherman Tree

At 275 feet tall and over 36 feet in diameter at the base, it is the largest living tree on Earth by volume. The surrounding Congress Trail showcases pristine sequoia groves.

Sequoia National Park 6,725 ft

Moro Rock Monolith

A sheer granite dome offering an expansive 360-degree overlook across the San Joaquin Valley and the snowy crests of the Great Western Divide.

Kings Canyon National Park 6,600 ft

General Grant Tree

Named the "Nation's Christmas Tree" by President Coolidge, this giant holds the second largest trunk volume and features the historic hollow Fallen Monarch nearby.

Kings Canyon (Cedar Grove) 4,600 ft

Zumwalt Meadow & Canyon Rim

Nestled at the bottom of the glacially carved canyon beside the Kings River, offering dramatic contrasts between golden autumnal meadows and vertical granite cliffs.

Sequoia National Park 6,500 ft

The Tunnel Log

A 275-foot long, 21-foot diameter fallen tree that was tunnelled in 1937 to permit cars to pass through. One of the most beloved road-trip photo ops.

Cedar Grove 4,700 ft

Roaring River Falls

A quick, paved walk brings you to this rushing emerald cascade carving into a granite chute, surrounded by vibrant fall foliage in October.

Trip Readiness

Mid-October Weather, Packing & Road Essentials

Layering (30°F to 65°F)

Sierra weather changes rapidly in mid-October. Morning hikes start around freezing (32°F–38°F), warming to a pleasant 60°F under afternoon sun.

  • • Moisture-wicking base layer
  • • Fleece mid-layer or lightweight down puffy
  • • Windproof/rain shell outer jacket
  • • Warm beanie & light touch-screen gloves

Tire Chains & Driving

By California law and park regulations, vehicles may be required to carry tire chains in vehicle trunks in mountain zones from mid-October onwards.

  • • Rent or buy tire chains that fit your tire size
  • • Top off gas at Fresno or Three Rivers
  • • Use low gear (L/2) when descending to prevent brake fade
  • • Watch for deer on Generals Highway at dusk

Zero-Cell Offline Survival

Mobile reception disappears completely once past Dunlap on CA-180 and past Three Rivers on CA-198. Do not rely on streaming GPS.

  • • Download offline Google Maps for Tulare & Fresno counties
  • • Download National Park Service (NPS) app offline content
  • • Store hotel booking confirmations as screenshots
  • • Bring portable battery power bank & car charger
